Introduction

Netflix is a prominent leader in the streaming industry and has contributed significantly to consumer entertainment preferences. In the early 2000s, Netflix was a competitor in the physical media distribution industry with competition like Redbox and Blockbusters. At the time, most businesses in this market focused on the short-term market and being profitable there. However, the transition to the digital age was a looming concern and Netflix responded proactively, introducing streaming services. Almost two decades later, Netflix is a global phenomenon that has revolutionized entertainment access on-the-go. Streaming companies such as Disney+, Hulu, Amazon Prime Video and Paramount, have entered the market due to this change in consumer needs and the documented success Netflix has created with its own business structure.

Starting in the first half of 2023, Netflix began to release “What We Watched” reports. They were convinced into doing so because advertisers needed to know the likelihood of viewers coming across thor content and Hollywood was concerned over whether creators were getting paid appropriately relative to viewership statistics for those projects. This project explores viewership and other features reported by Netflix from the first half of 2023 to the first half of 2026.

Data Acquisition

The data was acquired from Netflix’s online publications for their “What We Watched” reports. The reports were standardized across formatting and compiled into one collective data set which this project refers to as NETFLIX_DATA.

Data Preparation and Cleaning

To prepare the data for this project, two main actions were taken. First was converting column names into “tidy” ones. Some changes were not as significant and involved adding an underscore or making something lowercase, while others features that could potentially cause errors while coding. The second change made was a conversion of data types for year and available_globally. They were converted to integers and logical values respectively.

The updated variables and what they measure are listed here:

  • year: The year in which that entry was recorded.
  • period: This has one of seven values and represented either the first or second half a given year. For example, 2026H1 is the first half of 2026.
  • period_start: The date at which a given period starts.
  • period_end: The date at which a given period ends.
  • title: The name of some recorded movie or T.V. show.
  • runtime: Runtime is how long a title is (given in minutes).
  • hours_viewed: The total view time in hours for a title.
  • views: An approximate number of views for a title (hours_viewed/runtime).
  • release_date: The date at which a title became available.
  • available_globally: TRUE/FALSE value for whether a title is available around the world.
  • live_event: TRUE/FALSE value for whether a title was a live event.

Exploratory Questions

What date range is covered by this data set?

The date range covered by this dataset is January 01, 2023 to June 30, 2026. These values can be obtained by finding the earliest date in the period_start column and the latest date in the period_end column.

How many different shows and movies are covered in this data set?

There are 24,427 different shows and movies covered by this dataset. Grouping the data across title consolidates multiple entries for the same titles across different periods. Simply summarizing the number of rows after grouping results in how many shows and movies are present in the data.

How many total hours have been spent watching shows from the Bridgerton universe?

The total hours spent watching shows from the Bridgerton universe was 4,862,800,001. To find this value, NETFLIX_DATA was filtered by titles that contained the word “Bridgerton” and totaled across the hours_viewed for that subset.

How many total hours have been spent watching the five seasons of Stranger Things?

5,548,500,000 hours were spent watching the five seasons of Stranger Things. To find this value, NETFLIX_DATA filtered by including titles that contained the words “Stranger Things” and excluding those with words in the spin-off titles. For the final result, the hours_viewed were totaled across that subset.

What percentage of total watch time was spent on the Top 10 titles of 2026H1?

6.70% of the total watch time was spent on the Top 10 titles of 2026H1. To obtain this result, the sum of total hours_viewed for the top ten titles in 2026H1 were written as a fraction of the sum of total hours_viewed across all titles.

What fraction of Netflix’s catalog is globally available?

By title, 24.60% of Netflix’s catalog is globally available. To obtain this result, the data was grouped by title, filtered by TRUE values for available_globally and divided by the count total titles.

By hours_viewed, 51.92% of Netflix’s catalog is globally available. First, the data was filtered by TRUE values for available_globally and hours_viewed for that subset were aggregated. That value was then written as a fraction of hours_viewed across all the data.

Corporate Memo

Netflix continues to see success in viewership rates and performance across the last seven periods reported for the “What We Watched”. After analyzing specific areas for improvement, two main strategies for Netflix’s 2027 original content are recommended which are outlined below.

Global Availability vs. Regional Content

Recommendation: Netflix should release currently available regional titles globally or put effort toward global releases in general.

In terms of consumer preferences, there has been a significant shift in what consumers are watching. One of the most memorable examples of this was in 2021, when Netflix’s Korean drama series “Squid Game” became a global hit. However, this change was not overnight. Titles such as “Alice in Borderland” (Japan), “Money Heist” (Spain), and “Lupin” (France) have all been recognized internationally. Despite language barriers and cultural differences, the appreciation for these series lies in the stories. Therefore, I believe there should be a push to make regional content available globally or Netflix should produce more global content in general.

To obtain a general overview of this shift in preferences we can take a look at the snapshot below of the top 10 titles in terms of viewership grouped across global availability and titles. The global viewership base obtained from more widely accessible titles will stay committed to Netflix instead of competitors. For example, a viewer who enjoys a foreign genre may continue to stream on Netflix instead of a competitor if there were more options available in his/her region.

Short-Form vs. Long Form Content

Recommendation: For 2027, Netflix should contnue making original long form content, that is with runtime of ove 240 minutes.

A second question Netflix should consider for its 2027 strategy is whether short-form or long-form content is more valued. To analyze this issue, a filtering based on logical conditioning was run on the data. Netflix produces shows with an average of eight episodes that run for about 30 minutes. We can assume that the maximum runtime for short content has a runtime of 240 minutes or less. Anything else would be considered long form. This could include shows that have more episodes or longer episode times.

The idea is important to think about because in an era driven by social media, consumers’ attention span has considerably decreased. Reels are extremely popular and even fifteen seconds is sometimes not enough time to capture someone’s attention. It is possible we are experiencing a shift in preference towards short form content in the entertainment industry as well. However, the results of the below table show otherwise.
